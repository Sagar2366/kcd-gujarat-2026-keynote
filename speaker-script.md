### TAB: THE BAD

> *(Click Bad tab. Change tone — slower, deliberate.)*

Now let's talk about the things that should make us uncomfortable.

**Take does not equal Give.**

Thousands of Indian companies run their entire infrastructure on open source. Kubernetes, Prometheus, Envoy. They build billion-dollar products on free software. And how many contribute back? How many sponsor a meetup? How many let their engineers have even one day a month for upstream work?

> *(Pause. Let the room feel it.)*

**India has 2.25 million cloud native developers. Only about 5% are actively contributing to CNCF projects.**

The rest are consumers. We're known upstream for volume, not value. Transactional, low-effort PRs risk hurting our reputation. Not building it.

**Organizer pay? Zero.**

"How much does CNCF pay you?" — I get asked this all the time. The answer is zero. Organizers and volunteers spend their own time, their own money, their own weekends. We book venues, chase sponsors, set up chairs, and clean up after. All for free. The community runs on people who show up when nobody's watching.

**4,065 GSoC students. Number one in the world.**

But most disappear after the stipend. The bridge that was supposed to create lifelong contributors became a toll booth. As Aayush Saini from Red Hat, now a vLLM maintainer, said honestly — "The main hook is the stipend. After receiving it, we left."

> *(Scroll to secondary cards)*

**AI Spam PRs.** Maintainers are now closing more PRs than they merge. People raising pull requests generated entirely by AI without reading a single line of the codebase. Open source maintainers have become spam filters.

**Cert Black Market.** Exam dumps circulating on Telegram. Five certifications cleared without understanding a single concept. The badge says expert — the interview says otherwise.

**Codebase Frustration.** New contributors try to learn a massive codebase, get overwhelmed, get frustrated, and leave. Then they jump to another project and repeat. Na idhar ka, na udhar ka.

> *(Pause.)*

And finally — **Swag Culture.**

At KubeCon EU and Japan, halls are packed. People are standing in sessions because they want to learn. In India? People fighting over sponsor booths. A friend told me attendees looted an entire swag box at one event.

Here's the thing — when sponsors remember looted tables instead of packed talks, they don't come back. We lose the funding that makes events like today possible.

> *(Beat. Serious tone.)*

These are hard truths. But ignoring them won't make them go away.

### TAB: THE UNSPOKEN

> *(Click Unspoken tab. Quieter voice. More personal.)*

Now — the things nobody says out loud.

**Silent Sponsors.**

Nobody talks about this. Organizers and mentors silently spend money from their own pockets — paying for someone's conference ticket, covering travel so a first-timer can attend a big event. Because they know how important it is. During layoffs, people need networks. You get a reality check when you're in a room with people doing real work.

> *(Let that land.)*

Speak up, even if you fumble. People will judge — ignore that. The ones who matter will remember you showed up.

**Less than 4% women in cloud native India.**

Meetups, events, certifications, contributions — women are massively underrepresented everywhere. Less than 20 of 478 Kubestronauts are women. 3 of 42 Golden Kubestronauts. If the room doesn't look different, we're not building a community — we're building a club.

**Speaker Diversity.**

At CNCG Pune, we reserve at least one speaker slot for women and one for first-time speakers. Most meetups don't do this. If you don't make space on the stage, the stage stays the same faces forever.

**CFP Favoritism.**

Are CFPs always fair? There's talk of organizers selecting friends' proposals over better ones. People who know the right people get the stage. People who don't — never hear back. Blind reviews and clear evaluation criteria are the only way to earn trust in the process.

> *(Scroll to bottom cards)*

**Burnout Behind Smiles.** Organizers and ambassadors are burning out. But they can't say it because the community depends on them. They keep showing up because nobody else will. Nobody asks if they're okay.

**Vanishing Volunteers.** Join the group, add it to LinkedIn, attend one event, vanish. The same 5 people set up chairs and clean up after every single meetup.

**Tier-2 City Invisibility.** Everything happens in Bengaluru, Delhi, Pune, Mumbai. Engineers in smaller cities have no meetups, no mentors, no network. They exist — nobody builds for them.

**Consumers Who Never Give Back.** Companies run production on CNCF projects and never upstream a fix. Attendees show up for the food and swag, skip the talks, and add "KubeCon attendee" to LinkedIn.

> *(Pause. Then with conviction.)*

And this one — please remember this.

**Contribution is more than code.**

Nobody tells you this. Contributing to CNCF and cloud native can be any shape or size. Code, design, testing, teaching, conducting events, volunteering, social media, attendee management, poster design, website design, speaker management, F&B management, anchoring, CFP reviews, exam development, writing blogs, creating videos, testing features, adding new tests — and a hundred things nobody puts on a resume.

If you helped set up one chair at one meetup — you contributed. The ecosystem runs on people who show up, not just people who push code.

> *(Closing quote on screen)*

"We have the talent. We have the numbers. We have the community. What we need is each other."

## SLIDE 4 — YOUR MOVE

> *(Click to Your Move slide. Energy back up. Forward-looking.)*

Alright. You heard the good, the bad, and the unspoken. Now — your move.

I'm not going to tell you the community needs saving. It doesn't. I'm going to tell you it needs *you* — wherever you are.

> *(Point to cards as you go through them)*

**If you're an organizer** — reserve one speaker slot for women, one for first-timers. Run blind CFP reviews. And ask your volunteers if they're okay — not just if the venue is ready.

**If you're a maintainer** — write a real contributing guide. Not a template — a map. Label issues by complexity so newcomers don't drown. And review a first-timer's PR with patience, not gatekeeping.

**If you're a contributor** — stay after the stipend. Stay after the merged PR. Pick one project and commit for 6 months — not 6 repos in 6 weeks. And please — read the codebase before raising a PR.

**If you're a sponsor or a company** — give your engineers 20% time for upstream contributions. Sponsor a Tier-2 city meetup, not just Bengaluru. Fund travel for first-time conference attendees.

**If you're a student** — GSoC and LFX are the start, not the finish line. Attend a meetup in your city — or start one. Submit a CFP. Imposter syndrome lies. Your story matters.

**If you're an attendee** — stay for the talks, not just the swag table. Talk to a speaker after their session — it makes their day. And volunteer to help set up or clean up. Once. That's all it takes.

> *(Pause. Then the closing quote.)*

"The person who submitted her first PR five years ago now governs the entire ecosystem. Your first PR is waiting."

## DELIVERY NOTES

- **The Good**: Build energy. Be proud. Let the numbers land.
- **The Bad**: Slow down. Lower voice. Don't rush — discomfort is the point.
- **The Unspoken**: Most personal section. Speak from experience, not from slides.
- **Your Move**: Pick up energy again. Make eye contact with different parts of the room for each role.
- **Thank You**: Slow. Genuine. Don't read — deliver.
- **General**: Don't read cards verbatim. You know this stuff — the slides are reference points, not teleprompters. Talk TO the audience, not AT the screen.
- **Water**: Take a sip between tabs. It gives you a pause and the audience a moment.
- **Movement**: Walk across stage during The Good (energy). Stand still during The Bad (gravity). Move closer to audience during Unspoken (intimacy).
