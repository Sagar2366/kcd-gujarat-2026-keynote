The Hard Truths

Numbers that make us uncomfortable - but ignoring them won't make them go away.

Take ≠ Give
Corporates Consuming Without Contributing
Thousands of Indian companies run their entire infrastructure on open source - Kubernetes, Prometheus, Envoy. But how many give back? Corporate teams consume without contributing a single line upstream.
"They build billion-dollar products on free software, then won't sponsor a meetup or let their engineers contribute back."
5%
2.25M Devs, 5% Contributing
India has the world's largest cloud native developer base. Only 2,185 are actively contributing to CNCF projects. The rest are consumers - not builders. We're known upstream for volume, not value.
"Transactional, low-effort PRs risk hurting our reputation upstream."
$0
Organizer Pay
"How much does CNCF pay you?" Zero. Organizers and volunteers spend their own time, their own money, their own weekends. Nobody's getting rich running meetups.
"We book venues, chase sponsors, set up chairs, and clean up after - all for free. The community runs on people who show up when nobody's watching."
4,065
Stipend & Vanish
Indian GSoC students in 15 years - #1 globally. But most disappear after the stipend. The bridge that was meant to create lifelong contributors became a toll booth.
"The main hook is the Stipend. After receiving it, we left." - Aayush Saini, Red Hat, vLLM Maintainer
AI Spam PRs
Maintainers now close more PRs than they merge. People raising PRs generated by AI without reading a single line of the codebase. Open source maintainers have become spam filters.
Cert Black Market
Exam dumps circulating on Telegram. Five certifications cleared without understanding a single concept. The badge says expert - the interview says otherwise.
Codebase Frustration
New contributors try to learn a massive codebase, get overwhelmed, get frustrated, and leave. Then they jump to another project and repeat the cycle. Na idhar ka, na udhar ka.
"They come with energy, hit a wall of complexity, and silently disappear."
Swag Culture
  • KubeCon EU & Japan - halls packed, people standing in sessions
  • India - people fighting over sponsor booths
  • A friend told me attendees looted an entire swag box
  • When sponsors remember looted tables instead of packed talks - they don't come back

What Nobody Says Out Loud

These aren't accusations - they're patterns. And patterns only break when someone names them.

$$$
Silent Sponsors
Nobody talks about it, but organizers and mentors silently spend money from their own pockets - paying for someone's conference ticket, covering travel so a first-timer can attend a big event. Because they know how important it is. During layoffs, people need networks. You get a reality check. You meet the right people.
"Speak up, even if you fumble. People will judge - ignore that. The ones who matter will remember you showed up."
<4%
Women in Cloud Native India
Meetups, events, certifications, contributions - women are massively underrepresented everywhere. Less than 20 of 478 Kubestronauts. 3 of 42 Golden Kubestronauts.
"If the room doesn't look different, we're not building a community - we're building a club."
1 of ?
Speaker Diversity Gap
At CNCG Pune, we reserve at least one speaker slot for women and one for first-time speakers. Most meetups don't do this. If you don't make space on the stage, the stage stays the same faces.
?
CFP Favoritism
Are CFPs always fair? There's talk of organizers selecting friends' proposals over better ones. People who know the right people get the stage. People who don't - never hear back.
"Blind reviews and clear evaluation criteria are the only way to earn trust in the process."
Burnout Behind Smiles
Organizers and ambassadors are burning out - but they can't say it because the community depends on them. They keep showing up because nobody else will. Nobody asks if they're okay.
Vanishing Volunteers
Join the group, add it to LinkedIn, attend one event, vanish. The same 5 people set up chairs and clean up after every single meetup. Nobody else stays.
Tier-2 City Invisibility
Everything happens in Bengaluru, Delhi, Pune, Mumbai. Engineers in smaller cities have no meetups, no mentors, no network. They exist - but nobody builds for them.
Consumers Who Never Give Back
Companies run production on Kubernetes, Prometheus, Envoy - and never upstream a fix. Attendees show up for the food and swag, skip the talks, and add "KubeCon attendee" to LinkedIn. The community gives endlessly. The give-back ratio is embarrassing.
Contribution Is More Than Code
Nobody tells you this - contributing to CNCF and cloud native can be any shape or size. Code, non-code, design, testing, teaching, conducting events, volunteering, social media, attendee management, poster design, website design, speaker management, F&B management, anchoring, CFP reviews, exam development, writing blogs, creating videos, testing features, adding new tests, and a hundred things nobody puts on a resume. If you helped set up one chair at one meetup - you contributed. The ecosystem runs on people who show up, not just people who push code.
"We have the talent. We have the numbers. We have the community.
What we need is each other."

Your Move

You heard the good, the bad, and the unspoken. Now pick your role - and take one step.

As an Organizer
  • Reserve one speaker slot for women, one for first-timers
  • Run blind CFP reviews - no names, no companies
  • Ask your volunteers if they're okay, not just if the venue is ready
As a Maintainer
  • Write a real "contributing" guide - not a template, a map
  • Label issues by complexity so newcomers don't drown
  • Review a first-timer's PR with patience, not gatekeeping
As a Contributor
  • Stay after the stipend. Stay after the merged PR.
  • Pick one project and commit for 6 months - not 6 repos in 6 weeks
  • Read the codebase before raising a PR
As a Sponsor / Company
  • Give your engineers 20% time for upstream contributions
  • Sponsor a Tier-2 city meetup, not just Bengaluru
  • Fund travel for first-time conference attendees
As a Student
  • GSoC/LFX is the start, not the finish line
  • Attend a meetup in your city - or start one
  • Submit a CFP. Imposter syndrome lies. Your story matters.
As an Attendee
  • Stay for the talks, not just the swag table
  • Talk to a speaker after their session - it makes their day
  • Volunteer to help set up or clean up. Once. That's all it takes.
